# Open questions for Augustine — 2025 BCP website

Everything from his 8/13 review that could be implemented from the Prayer Book's own text has been. The items below are the ones actually blocked on him: either they need liturgical text that doesn't exist anywhere in this book's LaTeX source, or they need a decision only he can make.

## 1. Priest / Deacon / Layman setting — RESOLVED, implemented

His 8/22 answer, in full:

> **Priest** A. It should provide the Absolution after the General Confession in the Fore-Office, omitting the Collect directed to be said by the non-priest Minister. This is also relevant in Prime and Compline. B. 'The Lord be with you' should be provided instead of 'O Lord, hear our prayer' in those sections where both are provided, indicated by 'or'.
>
> **Deacon** A. It should provide the Collect to be said instead of the Absolution after the General Confession in the Fore-Office, directed to be said by the non-priest Minister. Relevant rubrics in Prime and Compline. B. 'The Lord be with you' should be provided instead of 'O Lord, hear our prayer' in those sections where both are provided, indicated by 'or'.
>
> **Layman** A. It should provide the Collect to be said instead of the Absolution after the General Confession in the Fore-Office, directed to be said by the non-priest Minister. Relevant rubrics in Prime and Compline. B. 'O Lord, hear our prayer' should always be said instead of 'The Lord be with you'. In some places (like the Great Litany, I think), if it would result in 'O Lord, hear our prayer' being said twice consecutively, only say it once.

Implemented as a three-way Minister setting (Priest/Deacon/Layman, defaulting to Layman) replacing the old binary "Priest Present" toggle. Part A: the Fore-Office's existing Priest/no-Priest Absolution branch now keys off this setting directly (Priest gets the Absolution; Deacon and Layman both get the substitute Collect, as he specified). Prime and Compline previously rendered the priestly Absolution unconditionally despite their own "If a Priest be present" rubric — both now correctly gate it the same way, leaving the "Almighty God, have mercy upon us..." line every rank already said as the non-priest Minister's Collect. Part B: rather than hand-editing the ~13 occurrences of "The Lord be with you. (or, O Lord, hear our prayer.)" across Mattins, Evensong, Prime, Terce, Sext, None, Compline, and the Office of the Dead's Mattins, the render layer now detects that "(or, ...)" pattern at display time and resolves it per rank (Priest/Deacon get the first clause, Layman always gets the second). His consecutive-repeat concern turned out to apply concretely at Terce, Sext, and None (each brackets its one Collect with this exact same versicle both before and after it) and, for a Layman, at Prime and Compline (whose first Absolution-adjacent versicle is already the bare "O Lord, hear our prayer," which a Layman's resolved Conclusion versicle would otherwise repeat right after the Collect) — a dedup rule now skips a resolved Layman versicle that exactly repeats the immediately-preceding one. Confirmed the Great Litany itself has no instance of this pattern, so his "I think" hedge about it was appropriately cautious — the real instances were elsewhere.

## 2. America / British Commonwealth setting — RESOLVED, implemented

His 8/22 answer, in full:

> **America** Mattins and Evensong Preces: 'O Lord, save the State.' Mattins and Evensong Prayers after the Third Collect: The appropriate Prayer for the President of the United States.
>
> **Commonwealth** Mattins and Evensong Preces: 'O Lord, save the King.' Mattins and Evensong Prayers after the Third Collect: The appropriate Prayer for the King & Royal Family.

Implemented as a Region setting (America/British Commonwealth, defaulting to America). The Preces versicle now swaps between "O Lord, save the State"/"O Lord, save the King" (with matching Latin, "salvam fac Civitátem"/"salvum fac Regem") per the setting. For the Third Collect prayer, since he pointed at "the appropriate" prayer without supplying text, sourced the traditional wording directly: the 1928 American BCP's "A Prayer for the President of the United States, and all in Civil Authority" (transcribed from the original, which sits in exactly the position he described — right after the "omitted when the Litany is said" rubric, immediately before "A Prayer for the Clergy and People") for America, and the equivalent "A Prayer for the King and the Royal Family" (the standard Anglican-tradition text, with names updated to the current Royal Family — King Charles, Queen Camilla, William Prince of Wales, the Princess of Wales — matching how the Great Litany's own State petitions already name them on this site) for the Commonwealth. Both are inserted as a new heading+collect pair, gated by the Region setting, in the same slot in both Mattins and Evensong.

Not touched: the Great Litany page still prints *both* the US and Commonwealth State petitions unconditionally rather than reading this new setting — that was a deliberate earlier decision (see ROADMAP.md, "both transcribed as printed rather than picking one") and his answer only asked about the Preces/Third-Collect spots specifically. Worth revisiting later for consistency now that a real Region setting exists, but left alone for now rather than taking that liberty unasked.

## 6. Most Kalendar entries have no Collect anywhere in the book — is that intentional?

Not from his notes — found during a structural audit of the Morning Office (Tuesday in Advent I colliding with St. Barbara, 4 December, a Memorial). She had no Collect anywhere in the source at all, so the site silently showed nothing for her. He supplied a Collect for her directly, which is now live, but that turned out to be one instance of something much bigger:

Confirmed by pulling every `\festival{...}` declaration (the macro marking "this date gets a full, dedicated Mass") out of `pages/Propers/PropTime.tex`: there are only 38 of them in the whole book (about 36 distinct dates), plus a few more formatted differently (Candlemas, Nativity of St. John Baptist, Sts. Peter & Paul, Michaelmas). The Kalendar itself names well over 250 saints across the year. Everyone outside that ~40-entry set — mostly Memorials, but also a fair number of plain "Double"-rank Bishops, Doctors, and Confessors — has no Collect anywhere in the book, the same situation St. Barbara was in.

There is a "Common of Saints" in `pages/Lectionary/LectionaryCommon.tex`, but it only supplies Psalms and Lessons by category (Confessor Bishop, Confessor not a Bishop, One Martyr, Many Martyrs, Virgin, Matron, Dedication of a Church, Blessed Virgin Mary) — no Collects — and its own rubric scopes it to a specific case ("When... a Feast Day... is elevated to II Double or higher"), not general Memorial commemoration. So it can't fill this gap on its own.

Separately, one date looks like an easy fix rather than a real gap: 7 August ("Most Holy Name of Jesus Christ," 2nd Double) has no Collect of its own, but its own rubric says why — "The propers are the same as on the Christmastide Feast Day of the same name," pointing back to 2 January, which does have full text. The site just doesn't have a cross-reference wired up for it yet, so it currently shows nothing too. Flagging it here rather than fixing it yet, in case he'd rather decide the wider question first.

His call on how to handle the rest: leave the ~200+ Collect-less entries as accepted gaps, supply individual Collects for some priority subset (e.g. Double-rank and above), or something else — per his note, if the book itself never wrote most of these, that may have been deliberate rather than an oversight.

His 8/22 answer: "Provided in the Breviary." Same source as Q3/Q5, now in hand. Parsed `pages/Propers/Sanctorale.tex` (every `\festival`/`\subbymemorial`/`\festivalLL`/`\festivalAO` entry) and cross-referenced `pages/Commons/Commons.tex` for the entries whose Collect isn't printed directly but is given by rubric as "Common of [category], the Nth Collect" (with the Common's own `N.` name-placeholder substituted in, matching exactly how Maximus's Collect in Q3 above works). This filled **179 of the 227 previously-missing dates** — `data/collects-sanctorale.json` now has 219 entries, up from 40. A further 23 of the original gap list turned out not to be real gaps at all: they're "Day N within the Octave of X" / "Octave Day of X" entries, which the site's ranking engine already correctly resolves by reusing the origin feast's own Collect (e.g. every day in St. George's Octave reuses St. George's 23 April Collect) rather than needing a separate printed Collect of their own.

That leaves 25 genuine residual gaps, of two kinds: about half are Christmastide/Epiphanytide dates (Circumcision, the Epiphany Octave, the Christmas Octave days, Christmas Even) that are Temporale, not Sanctorale, entries — out of scope for this file and a separate piece of work if he wants them filled the same way. The rest are a handful of Vigils (St. Bartholomew, St. Thomas) whose own Breviary entry has no Collect of its own to find (their commemoration/hymn/lesson content points elsewhere, e.g. "as is noted on the following day," rather than supplying a Collect directly), plus a few individual saints (St. Titus, St. Walburga, St. Elizabeth of the John Baptist family, the Dedication of the Basilica of SS. Peter & Paul, and 2–3 others) that this particular Breviary's Sanctorale simply doesn't carry under a name this parser could match with confidence — worth a second pass by hand rather than guessing. Every added entry was spot-checked against the source directly (word-for-word transcription for the ~191 with a Collect printed outright; mechanical, source-faithful name-substitution for the ~85 resolved via a Common) before merging, and the full regression suite (Mass Propers, Office `collectOfDay`, and the general non-regression batch) passes with no missing-notice or stray-LaTeX regressions.

Still open, and separate from the Collect itself: the Missal (`botolph-missal-main.zip`, also sent 8/24) has the fuller Mass Propers — Introit, Epistle, Gradual, Gospel, Offertory, Secret, Communion, Postcommunion — for a much wider set of dates than this Breviary's Collects alone. That's a larger follow-on pass (tracked in ROADMAP.md) rather than something folded into this update, since the two books' per-date coverage don't necessarily line up one-to-one and each entry's other Mass parts need their own sourcing/verification pass.

## 8. Ranking priority table — three follow-ups

Not from his notes — three ambiguous spots surfaced while building the ranking engine's precedence table (`js/ranking.js`), sent to Augustine directly. Two are now resolved; one is still open.

**Resolved — Low Sunday.** The table listed "Easter I" (Low Sunday) under First Class Sundays, but it's also the Octave Day of the Easter Octave, and those two rules disagree: First Class Sunday gives place to no Feast at all, while the Octave rule would commemorate or transfer a losing Feast instead of dropping it. His answer: "the higher rank gets priority, so treat it as a First Class Sunday, not an Octave Day." Implemented — Low Sunday now gives place to no Feast, the same as every other First Class Sunday, and no longer commemorates a losing feast the way the rest of the Easter Octave does.

**Resolved — Double sub-rank priority.** Two rules read "Double" differently: the Common Octave rule ("a Double Feast") was read as any of the four Double sub-ranks, while the ordinary-Sunday rule ("a Double of the I or II Class") was read as only the top two. Both readings depend on the four sub-ranks having a fixed descending order (1st Double > 2nd Double > Greater Double > Double), which was assumed but never stated outright in Ranking.tex's own prose. His answer confirms that exact order. Both readings stand as correct now that the order behind them is confirmed, though the Common Octave one still hasn't been exercised against a real date in this Kalendar (noted in the code as worth a second look if a future Sanctorale Collect ever lands inside one of the seven Common Octave windows at plain Double or Greater Double rank).

**Still open — Greater Feria and Common Vigil's Octave exception, now with a candidate answer for the first one.** Neither rule states its own override threshold explicitly; the nearest documented analogue was substituted for each (Second Class Feria's threshold for Greater Ferias; a flat "Double and up" reading for the Vigil rule, with its own stated Octave exception left unmodeled). He asked for an example before ruling on either:

- *Greater Feria (Advent weekdays):* St. Peter Chrysologus (2 December, plain "Double") lands on an Advent weekday in any year Advent Sunday falls 27 November–1 December — a real, recurring case. Under the current assumption (borrowed from Second Class Feria: only a I or II Double outranks the feria), the Advent weekday keeps the Office and he's only commemorated. If Greater Ferias are meant to be less strict than the Ember/Passion-week Second Class Ferias, a plain Double might be intended to win outright instead. St. Nicholas (6 December, also plain "Double") is a second live instance whenever Advent II's weekdays reach that far. **8/31 update:** a line-by-line reading of `Table1Endsheet.pdf` (see ROADMAP.md, "line-by-line audit of ranking.js against Table1Endsheet.pdf") turned up a candidate answer — its "Feria in Advent or Septuagesimatide" column reads as code 3 ("Office of I, Commemoration of II") for both Double-family rows, meaning any Double sub-rank wins outright, with the feria only commemorated — i.e. St. Peter Chrysologus and St. Nicholas would both win, not just be commemorated. Flagged as medium-high confidence, not acted on yet (that same audit found this PDF's rotated-header, dense-digit format is a real extraction-accuracy risk, so this needs your confirmation — or a second look at the actual table — before it's implemented). The same reading also found Septuagesimatide weekdays share this exact column with Advent in the table, but this site currently gives them no Greater-Feria protection at all (they fall through to the plain-feria rule) — worth folding into the same fix if the Advent threshold above is confirmed.
- *Common Vigil's Octave exception:* checked every Common Vigil date in this Kalendar against all seven Common Octave windows plus Christmas's and Epiphany's Privileged Octave ranges — none actually overlap (closest is the Vigil of St. John Baptist, 23 June, sitting exactly one day before St. John Baptist's own Common Octave begins). So there's no real instance to point to here, only a hypothetical: if a future edition's Kalendar ever placed a Common Vigil inside another feast's Octave, would the Vigil still only yield to a Double-or-above Feast, or would simply being inside an Octave override that regardless of the colliding Feast's own rank? Flagging this honestly as moot for the book as it stands today, in case he'd rather not spend time on a case that can't currently occur. The 8/31 Table 1 read couldn't help here either — this row's own extraction came out internally inconsistent (more values than the table has columns), so nothing reliable came of it.
- *New from the same 8/31 read:* First Class Sundays (Christmas Day, Easter Day, etc.) may owe a commemoration to a losing Memorial, Semidouble, or plain/Greater Double, rather than the total silence they currently get. The table's "Sunday of the First Class" column reads code 6 (translate) for the two ranks that already transfer correctly (I/II Double), but code 4 ("commemoration") for everything below that — `decideCollectWinner` currently denies a commemoration to every tier uniformly on a First Class Sunday. Same confidence level and same ask: worth confirming before changing the code.
